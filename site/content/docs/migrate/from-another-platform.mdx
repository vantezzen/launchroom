---
title: Move apps from another platform
description: Move apps one at a time from your old platform to launchroom without downtime. Variables, Compose apps, databases and files, then the DNS switch. Umami as the worked example.
---

You move one app at a time. Your old platform keeps serving each app until you switch its DNS, so the app stays online the whole time. For every app:

1. Deploy it on launchroom.
2. Copy its variables.
3. Import its database and files.
4. Check it on its generated address.
5. Switch DNS.
6. Remove it from your old platform.

The examples move **Umami** (website analytics on PostgreSQL). The same steps work for any app with a PostgreSQL, MySQL or MongoDB database and files on disk.

## Before you start

- [Install launchroom](/docs/getting-started/install) on a server.
- **Lower the DNS TTL** of every domain you'll move to 300 seconds or less, at least as long before the switch as the current TTL (often 1 hour or 1 day). Resolvers then pick up the switch within minutes, and you can switch back as fast.
- Keep shell access to your old server. The commands below run there with `docker exec`.

<Steps>
<Step>
### Deploy the app

How depends on how it runs on your old platform:

- **A common app** such as Umami: open **New project**, choose **Template** and pick it. The Umami template adds Umami and a managed PostgreSQL 17 database, connects them, generates `APP_SECRET` and deploys.
- **A `docker-compose.yml`**: [import the Compose file](/docs/projects/templates-and-compose#import-a-compose-file). Well-known database images become managed databases, so you can import their data in the next steps.
- **Your own code**: [import the repository](/docs/getting-started/first-deploy). launchroom detects how to build it.

Pick the same database engine and the same or a newer major version as on your old platform: a dump only imports into the same or a newer version.

On its first start, Umami creates its tables. That's expected: the import replaces them.
</Step>
<Step>
### Copy the variables

Write the app's variables to a `.env` file on your old server:

```sh
docker exec umami env > umami.env
```

Remove what launchroom sets itself: `PATH`, `HOSTNAME`, `HOME`, the database's address and credentials (`DATABASE_URL`, `DB_HOST` and similar come from the connection), and anything the image sets itself (`NODE_VERSION` and similar).

Open the service's **Variables** tab and paste the file or choose **Import .env**. launchroom shows a preview with conflicts and suggests which values to keep as secrets. Deploy to apply them. From a terminal, `launchroom env set KEY=VALUE…` adds variables (staged) and `launchroom deploy --staged` applies them.

#### Umami's secrets

Umami 3.4 reads one secret, `APP_SECRET`. When it isn't set, Umami derives it from `DATABASE_URL`. It signs sign-in tokens, share links and the tracker's cache tokens, and it is part of how Umami hashes visitor sessions. The data doesn't depend on it, but with a new secret everyone signs in again, shared links stop working and visitors in the current month count as new sessions. Copy these from your old server when they are set:

- `APP_SECRET`
- `TWO_FACTOR_ENCRYPTION_KEY`: Umami encrypts two-factor secrets with it. Without it, users with two-factor sign-in can't sign in.
- `SALT_ROTATION`, if you changed it.

If your old app had no `APP_SECRET`, its secret came from its old `DATABASE_URL`. Keep the generated one; everyone signs in once more. Older Umami versions used `HASH_SALT`. Umami 3.4 doesn't read it.

<Callout title="Umami 1.x">
Umami 2 changed the database schema. If your old platform runs Umami 1, upgrade it there with Umami's v1 to v2 migration before you dump the database.
</Callout>
</Step>
<Step>
### Import the database

Find the database container and its user and database name:

```sh
docker ps --format '{{.Names}}\t{{.Image}}'
docker exec umami-db env | grep -E '^(POSTGRES|MYSQL|MONGO)'
```

Dump it with the engine's own tool. The dump streams to a file on your old server:

```sh
# PostgreSQL: custom format, compressed
docker exec umami-db pg_dump -Fc -U umami umami > umami.dump

# MySQL or MariaDB (password from the container's env)
docker exec shop-db sh -c 'exec mysqldump --single-transaction --routines --triggers -u"$MYSQL_USER" -p"$MYSQL_PASSWORD" "$MYSQL_DATABASE"' | gzip > shop.sql.gz

# MongoDB
docker exec chat-db mongodump --archive --gzip --db chat -u app -p '<password>' --authenticationDatabase admin > chat.archive
```

To dump inside the container instead, write to `/tmp` and copy it out: `docker exec umami-db pg_dump -Fc -U umami -f /tmp/umami.dump umami`, then `docker cp umami-db:/tmp/umami.dump .`. Copy the dump to the machine you upload from, for example with `scp`.

On launchroom, open the database on the canvas and go to **Backups → Move data → Import data…**. Drop the dump in. The upload resumes if your connection drops. launchroom shows what it found ("PostgreSQL custom dump · from PostgreSQL 15.8") and what the database holds now.

Umami already created its tables, so choose **Replace everything**. launchroom takes a safety snapshot, stops Umami, drops and recreates the database, imports the dump and starts Umami again. Type the database's name to confirm.

From a terminal, with a token that has the `data:transfer` ability:

```sh
launchroom db import postgres umami.dump --replace
```

When the old database is reachable from the internet, **From a connection URL** copies it directly without a dump file. See [Import and export](/docs/databases/import-and-export) for formats, version rules and what launchroom adjusts in a dump.
</Step>
<Step>
### Import files

If the app keeps uploads or other files on disk, add a volume at the same path on the service (**Settings → Volumes**) and deploy. Then pack the folder on your old server:

```sh
docker exec app tar -czf - -C /app/uploads . > uploads.tar.gz
```

In the service's **Backups** tab, choose **Import files…** next to the volume and upload `uploads.tar.gz`. Owners and modes from the archive are kept, or set one owner for all files if the image runs as a non-root user. See [Import files into a volume](/docs/databases/import-and-export#import-files-into-a-volume).
</Step>
<Step>
### Check the app

Open the app on its generated address. For Umami: sign in with your old account and check that your websites and their history are there. Check the service's logs for errors.
</Step>
<Step>
### Switch DNS

[Add your domain](/docs/projects/domains-and-tls#add-a-custom-domain) to the service and point its DNS record at launchroom. launchroom issues a certificate once the record resolves. With a low TTL, traffic moves within minutes. The Umami tracking script on your sites keeps working because the domain stays the same.

Requests that reach your old platform between the dump and the switch stay there. To move without that gap, stop the app on your old platform, take a fresh dump, import it again with **Replace everything** and switch DNS right after.
</Step>
<Step>
### Clean up

Once traffic reaches launchroom (check the service's metrics) and you're sure:

- Stop the app on your old platform, and keep its last dump until launchroom has taken its first scheduled backups.
- Raise the DNS TTL again.
- Remove `umami.env` and the dump files from your machines: they hold secrets and data.
</Step>
</Steps>
